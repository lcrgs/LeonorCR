Brief

Lefta Wealth is a wealth management bank with over 100 years of heritage that has fallen behind online. For the UX Design Project of my MSc at Glasgow Caledonian University, I designed a Stocks & Shares ISA app that feels secure, simple and personal: attracting younger investors without alienating the loyal ones.

Roles

- UX Researcher & Designer (Solo Project)
- Led the full Double Diamond process: Discover, Define, Develop, Deliver.
- Designed the visual identity, design system and high-fidelity prototype.
- Ran user testing and iterated on the feedback.

Research

- The insight: The problem isn't trust, but translation. Lefta needs to turn its trust and expertise into a digital experience.
- 18-30s: Only 23% are willing to invest. 66% find it too risky and 44% feel they lack the knowledge. They learn on YouTube and TikTok.
- 40-60s: 79% use mobile banking, but 62% still trust traditional banks over fintechs and want access to a real person.
- Competitors: Heuristic evaluations and user journey maps of Wealthify, Moneybox and Revolut revealed disappearing navigation, fake buttons and heavy financial jargon.

Challenges & Strategy

Designing for two very different audiences: young adults paralysed by fear of investing, and established clients who are sceptical of digital tools.

- Solution: Created three personas (Ben, Jane and David), 54 POVs and HMW questions, and prioritised features with the Kano Model and MoSCoW.
- Solution: A guided onboarding that turns fears into simple choices and builds the user's first personalised, goal-based plan.
- Solution: Plain language instead of jargon, TikTok-style learning, a risk-free simulator and an AI smart chat.
- Solution: Visible trust through a security dashboard, accessibility settings and confirmation steps for critical actions.

Results & Impact

- Brand identity: A new logo that deconstructs Lefta's fleur-de-lis into three rectangles, forming an "L": heritage translated into a modern language.
- Tested & refined: Low and high-fidelity prototypes were tested with users and improved from their feedback.
- Key learning: UX design is more about translation than creating pretty screens: business goals into user benefits, anxieties into successful interactions and brand values into visuals.

Brand

The logo deconstructs a traditional fleur-de-lis into three simple rectangles: heritage translated into a modern language. The empty space forms an "L", for Lefta, through the Gestalt principle of closure.

What Testing Changed

Final testing surfaced small details that matter: an "X" on the agreement checkbox read as negative, and a toggle's colour change wasn't obvious enough. Testers found the balance of text worked for all ages, but younger users wanted more visuals.
