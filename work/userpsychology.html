Brief

An academic portfolio for the Applied User Psychology module of my MSc at Glasgow Caledonian University. Over eleven weeks, I explored how psychology shapes the way people use products, applying each topic to a real-world design, and finished with an essay on the persuasive design of the focus app Opal.

Roles

- Researcher & Writer (Solo Project)
- Wrote eight weekly assignments, an essay and a self-reflection.
- Designed the portfolio layout and infographic CV.

Weekly Assignments

- Persuasion: How the Lidl Plus app uses scratch cards, progress bars and countdowns to drive spending.
- Learning: How Apple Pay's sound uses classical conditioning to make spending feel rewarding.
- Memory: The low fuel light as an external aid for prospective memory.
- Gestalt: Metro banners and Portuguese tiles.
- Emotional Design: Bordallo Pinheiro's ceramics through Don Norman's three levels.

Essay: The Persuasive Architecture of Opal

Opal is a focus app that uses the same psychology behind compulsive phone use to help people reclaim their focus.

- Friction: Applies B.J. Fogg's Behaviour Model, making distraction harder and focus the easiest choice.
- Commitment & Scarcity: Uses Cialdini's principles and loss aversion through goals and streaks.
- Social Proof: Leaderboards turn focus into a shared norm.
- Variable Rewards: Collectible milestone gems sustain long-term motivation.

Reflection

- Key insight: Psychology in design isn't bad in itself; it is a tool. The goal should be to understand users, not to control them, but to help them.
- Personal growth: I learned that psychology is present everywhere, that consistency is the most important investment we can make, and the importance of stopping to think about why we act a certain way.
